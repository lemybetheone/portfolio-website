Built solo dbtAirflowS3Postgres

Formula 1 analytics pipeline

Every Formula 1 season since 1950, pulled from a rate-limited public API, landed immutably in S3, loaded into Postgres, and modelled into a tested Kimball star schema with dbt. Orchestrated nightly by Airflow, served through Metabase.

The part worth reading.One dbt test derives each championship total from the race and sprint facts and compares it against the official standings. It failed — 437 official points against 399 derived for the 2024 season — because sprint points were missing entirely. The test found a defect a row count never would have.

Why the full history mattered.Backfilling seventy-seven seasons takes about seven and a half hours against a 500-request-per-hour budget, and it is the reason the data has anything to say: DNF rates fall from 49% in the 1950s to 13% today, with a bump in the 1980s exactly where the turbo era belongs.

Decisions are written down.Sprint results are a separate fact table rather than a session_type column, because a fact whose grain needs a filter to be correct is a reliable source of wrong numbers. The alternatives that lost are logged in the repository.

03 — how i work

  1. Nothing reaches production by hand.Ingestion, infrastructure, modelling and transformation live in separate version-controlled repositories, each under pull-request review with continuous integration.
  2. Every model declares and tests its grain.The tests worth writing are business rules, not structural ones — a reconciliation test found a real defect that a row count would have passed.
  3. Loads are idempotent and replayable.An immutable landing zone means the warehouse can be rebuilt without re-spending an API budget, and re-running any step changes nothing.
  4. Pipelines report their own failures.Scheduled, retried per task, with failed records captured rather than silently dropped.
  5. Decisions are written down.What was chosen, what was rejected, and why — so the reasoning survives the person who had it.
